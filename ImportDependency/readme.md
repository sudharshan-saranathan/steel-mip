### Import Dependence Study

This study evaluates how **India's steel decarbonization pathway** changes under different levels of **coking coal** and **natural gas (NG)** import availability. Unlike the base model, **fuel prices remain fixed** while only **fuel availability** is varied, isolating the effect of import dependence on technology adoption, system cost, emissions, and import expenditure.

#### Study Configuration

The model evaluates:

* **4 import regimes** formed by combining:

  * Abundant / Scarce **Coking Coal**
  * Abundant / Scarce **Natural Gas**
* Multiple **H₂-DRI start years**
* Fixed cumulative emissions cap of **1.8 tCO₂/t steel**

#### Import Availability Assumptions

**Coking Coal**

* India imports approximately **90%** of its coking coal requirement in the base year.
* Domestic blendable coking coal production grows by **7.5% per year** in all scenarios.
* **Abundant:** imports continue to grow at **6.4% per year**.
* **Scarce:** imports remain fixed at **54.5 Mt/year**, representing prolonged import stagnation.

**Natural Gas**

* The steel sector receives **10%** of national natural gas availability.
* Domestic gas supply remains constant at **2.67 Mt/year** (50% of the 2025 allocation).
* **Abundant (Policy):** national gas availability increases to **32.2 Mt** by 2050.
* **Scarce (Business-as-Usual):** national gas availability increases only to **10.7 Mt** by 2050.

#### Model Outputs

For each import regime and hydrogen adoption year, the model reports:

* Levelized steel production cost
* Total emissions
* Cumulative coking coal and natural gas import expenditure
* Cumulative natural gas imports
* 2050 steel production shares (BF-BOF, Coal DRI, NG DRI, H₂ DRI, and Scrap EAF)
* Hydrogen emission reduction in 2050
* CCS deployment in 2050

Together, these results quantify how **fuel supply security** influences India's least-cost pathway for steel sector decarbonization while keeping all other model assumptions unchanged.
